
# The Long Way Out — GM Prep
### Season 3, Episode 1 — Minos Cluster · Transit + Arrival

## Overview

**Time:** Immediately following Episode 8 — Escape from Tatooine. Hyperspace transit of roughly five days, arriving at Travnin, sector capital of the Minos Cluster.
**Tone:** A breather after the manhunt's tight clock — quiet, character-focused transit beats, followed by a tense but bureaucratic (not violent) customs encounter. Think *first day somewhere nobody knows your name.*
**Goal:** Get the crew off the Empire's radar, get the *Centennial Duck* properly repaired, and open the door to life in the Cluster.

**What the players know:**
- They escaped Tatooine, but barely — the *Duck*'s transponder and their faces are now known to the Empire
- [Vytor Shrike](../npcs/vytor-shrike.md) has a lead: the Minos Cluster, about five days out, where nobody will recognize them and "no questions asked" freight work pays well
- The ship took real damage in the escape — repairs aren't optional

**What the players don't know:**
- The Cluster's customs service is thin but not toothless — Babel Torsch runs it personally and takes his job seriously
- They're arriving broke — real repairs mean real debt, and the Cluster's credit effectively comes from one source: Yerkys ne Dago
- Imperial Security Bureau assets are scattered quietly through the Cluster, independent of local Imperial command — a description matching the *Duck*'s crew could reach the wrong ears eventually

---

## Scene One — Five Days to Anywhere

The jump to Travnin takes about five days — the longest stretch of downtime the crew's had since Kwenn Space Station. Let this breathe.

**Good material for this scene:**
- Fallout from the Tusken Fort reveal — Bora and Indie processing that Graff Raster is their uncle, if that scene has landed by now
- Torbek's situation: Jabba's reach doesn't extend this far, and he knows it. First real chance to exhale.
- Ship repairs in transit — Gune (and Eck, if he pitches in) buys time and reduces the repair bill waiting at Travnin
- Graff and Vytor Shrike swapping stories about the Cluster from the privateer days — a good place to drop the Grand Design bar, Porgo Goo, or other Cluster color as rumor before the players arrive

**Mechanical option:** A Moderate *starship repair* roll from Gune during transit reduces the credits needed at Travnin — suggested 300–500 credits' worth of parts saved, GM's call.

## Scene Two — Travnin Customs

Travnin is a windswept moon orbiting a gas giant, the Cluster's Imperial sector capital — worn, bureaucratic, and visibly past its prime. Every ship gets boarded and inspected before it's cleared dirtside.

> *The moon's the color of an old bruise, lit sideways by the gas giant it orbits. A customs corvette hails you before you've even found the beacon. "Unidentified freighter, cut engines and prepare to be boarded. This is Customs Officer Babel Torsch."*

**[Babel Torsch](../npcs/babel-torsch.md)** personally boards the *Duck* — he does this for every new arrival, no exceptions. He is meticulous, humorless, and cannot be bribed. He does not — yet — know who they are. He *will* notice anything that doesn't add up: a mismatched cargo manifest, an unregistered weapon, a lightsaber-shaped bulge under a robe.

### Possible Approaches

**1. Straight and Clean**
File an honest (if thin) manifest and let Torsch do his inspection. Slow, boring, safe — no rolls needed unless something specific is being hidden.

**2. The Cover Story**
Claim a plausible reason for being this far out (independent traders, following work). Moderate *con* to sell it. Torsch isn't hunting liars specifically — he's hunting paperwork that doesn't match cargo.

**3. Hiding Something**
If Graff's lightsaber, weapons beyond declared limits, or anything Rebel-adjacent is aboard: Moderate to Difficult *sneak* or *con* to keep it out of Torsch's inspection. Failure doesn't mean arrest outright — Torsch issues a citation (a minor infraction and a fine) and flags the ship for a follow-up inspection later in the season. Save a catastrophic failure for something that actually matters.

**Torsch's manner:** Polite, procedural, utterly unmoved by bluster or bribery. *"I suggest you recalculate your load, or else you could be found guilty of defrauding the Imperial Office of Customs."* He is not the villain of this scene — he's a wall the players have to get around, and he'll be back.

## Scene Three — Squaring the Debt

Repairs at Travnin aren't cheap, and the crew isn't flush. Two options — either or both:

**[Yerkys ne Dago](../npcs/yerkys-ne-dago.md)** — the Cluster's biggest loan shark and crime boss. Fronts the credits for repairs at real interest. Gracious, even warm, in person — the threat is all implication. Fastest option, and the one that puts the crew in his debt for the rest of the season.

**Porgo Goo** *(mentioned, not yet statted)* — a Chortose engineer running an off-the-books repair shop. Cheaper, faster, and he'll skip payment entirely for something "interesting" — an illegal mod, a favor, a good story. Good option if the GM wants to delay the Yerkys debt hook, or to run both: some repairs financed, some bartered.

---

## End Beat

Ship repaired (or repair scheduled), crew quietly in debt, and Travnin's rumor mill already reaching them — word of steady cargo work at a place called **the Grand Design**, the bar where every deal in the Cluster starts.

> *Torsch clears you for planetfall. As the boarding ramp lowers onto Travnin's grey tarmac, Vytor Shrike nods toward the skyline. "Told you. Nobody out here cares who you used to be."*

**Fade to Episode 2 — the Grand Design.**

| Action | Difficulty |
|---|---|

| Hide contraband/weapons from inspection | Moderate–Difficult *sneak* or *con* |
| Reduce repair costs via in-transit work | Moderate *starship repair* |
| Negotiate better terms with Yerkys ne Dago | Moderate *bargain* (softens the debt, doesn't remove it) |
