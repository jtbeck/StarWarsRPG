Campaign Rules: Heat and Infractions

Heat and Infractions

Wanted Levels, Bounties and Imperial Law

Breaking the Law

Sooner or later, every crew of spacers runs afoul of the law. A blown alarm, a security holocam nobody thought to check, a guard who wakes up with a headache and a very good memory of your face: any of these can follow a character long after the job is done.

The Heat Index tracks how badly each character is wanted, and by whom. Heat is rated separately for each jurisdiction: a city, a crime lord's organization, or the Galactic Empire itself. A character can be clean in one place and hunted in another.

Heat Levels

Each jurisdiction where a character is wanted has a Heat rating from 0 to 3.

HeatEffect
0○○○ Clean. Nobody's looking.
1●○○ Noticed. A small local bounty is posted. Make a recognition roll (see below), triggering on a 1.
2●●○ Wanted. The bounty doubles. Patrols check IDs. Recognition triggers on a 1–2. Imperial authorities get involved.
3●●● Hunted. Real bounty hunters take the job. Recognition triggers on a 1–3, and the authorities actively search for the character instead of waiting to stumble onto them.

Recognition Rolls

Whenever a character with Heat is in a public place in that jurisdiction, the gamemaster rolls 1D. If the result falls within the character's trigger range, someone has recognized them: a cop, an amateur bounty hunter, or a snitch hoping to sell a tip. What happens next depends on who spotted them, and how quickly the character notices.

Raising Heat

Heat goes up when a character is loud again in the same jurisdiction: a firefight, another alarm, a dead officer. The bigger the incident, the faster it climbs.

Clearing Heat

How Heat gets cleared depends on the situation: who wants the character, what they did, and who they know. Paying a fine to the local authority is the usual legal route, and the authority sets the price. In Mos Eisley, that means Prefect Depp, who is sure to make it as humiliating as possible.

Heat can also be bribed down, worked off with a favor, forged away with new identity papers, or outrun by leaving the system. Outrunning it doesn't make the bounty go away, though. It just means nobody local is looking … for now.

Rap Sheets

Every character keeps a Rap Sheet: a running record of infractions and penalties, plus a Heat rating for each jurisdiction where they are wanted.

Add an entry whenever the crew breaks the law somewhere that notices, and update it when a fine gets paid or things escalate.
