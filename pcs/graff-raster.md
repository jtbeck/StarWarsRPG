## Description

An aging man gone to seed — wild, unkempt gray hair, a scraggly beard, wrapped in a threadbare old robe over a faded shirt, like he hasn't seen a mirror in years. When the PCs first see him, he is seated at a massive pipe organ in his music room at [Tusken Fort](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Tusken_Fort), sipping from a long glass. Shambling and unhurried on the surface, but his eyes are sharp, stern, missing nothing — the old authority is still there underneath, and it surfaces the moment he speaks. The room around him is an exotic musical museum — instruments from across the galaxy, the one orderly thing about him.

It's hard to square with the stories — the privateer who talked his way out of Separatist boarding parties as often as he fought his way out, all flourish and nerve, more legend than man. That man is still buried in here somewhere, under the beard and the drink, and he surfaces without warning: a dramatic flourish, a wink, a boast — before Graff catches himself and sinks back into the gloom.

He raises his glass and says simply: *"And now the hiding ends."*

## Background

Graff Raster was a [Jedi Knight](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Jedi/Legends) during the [Clone Wars](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Clone_Wars/Legends). He didn't fall to the dark side — he walked away from the Order on principle. He'd found evidence the Banking Clan was quietly bankrolling both sides of the war, Republic and Separatist alike, and when the Council wouldn't act on it, he decided he wasn't going to keep fighting and dying in a war somebody else was profiting from both ends of. So he left.

Free of the Order — and with nowhere else to point a lightsaber he no longer trusted himself to carry — he took a Separatist letter of marque and turned privateer, flying under a commission from **General Cracken**, preying on Republic shipping across the Rim. He was loud and theatrical about it, more showman than fugitive. History remembers the winners: the Separatists lost, so the histories call him a pirate instead of a privateer. [Quist](../npcs/quist.md) ran with his crew through those years.

Cracken remembers Graff fondly and remembers the commission as legitimate. Years later, when Cracken's rebel cell learned Graff was alive on Tatooine, he knew exactly who to send after him: **Captain Bora**, who served under his command during the war — and who also happens, unknowingly, to be Graff's own nephew. Cracken picked Bora for both reasons. Bora only knows about one of them.

Nobody came looking for the ex-Jedi privateer, not during the war and not after the Empire rose in its place — which is its own quiet mystery Graff has never solved. Eventually he got tired of the life anyway and settled on Tatooine to build something quieter, calling in old crew — [Shrike](../npcs/vytor-shrike.md) and [Jungen](../npcs/jungen.md) — who were willing to go quiet with him.

That quiet held for years. Then [stormtroopers](https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Stormtrooper/Legends) came crawling across the desert, and whatever kept the Empire off his back all this time finally ran out. He called Shrike and Jungen back in and began to prepare.

He did not count on betrayal from within. [Quist](../npcs/quist.md) — his old partner from the privateer years — sold his location to the Empire.

## Personality

Cynical, foul-mouthed, and pessimistic — but with a heart of gold underneath the wreckage. Years of quiet living and drinking have worn the old showman's swagger down to a rasp, but it's still there when he needs it, and it still slips out sideways when he's not paying attention.

## Objectives

To protect the family he never told he had, and to make up — somehow — for a lifetime spent hiding instead of fighting. He isn't sure redemption is still on the table. He intends to find out before the Empire decides for him.

#### Force Powers Reference

*Quick reference for play. Full rules in the cited sourcebook. Prerequisite chains are tracked so nothing on this list is learned out of order: **Life Detection → Danger Sense → Combat Sense**, and **Control Pain → Reduce Injury**. Concentration has no prerequisite.*

**Control**

| Power | Difficulty | Prerequisite | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Concentration** | Easy (calm) / Difficult (angry, afraid) / Very Difficult (acting on those feelings) | — | Clears the mind for one task: on success, +4D to a single action this round. Nothing else may be done that round — no other skill use, no dodge/parry. One round only, can't be kept up. |
| **Control Pain** | Very Easy (stunned/wounded) / Easy (incapacitated) / Difficult (mortally wounded) | — | Can be kept up. Lets Graff act without the die-code penalties of his actual injury level — the injury is still real, and re-rolled at the new difficulty every time he's hurt again. |
| **Reduce Injury** | Moderate (incapacitated) / Difficult (mortally wounded) / Very Difficult (dead) | Control Pain | Used the round of injury or the round after. On success, costs a Force Point and downgrades the injury to wounded (a would-be-fatal hit still leaves a permanent injury). |

**Sense**

| Power | Difficulty | Prerequisite | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Life Detection** | Very Easy if the target is Force-sensitive, Moderate otherwise | — | Can be kept up. Detects living sentients within 10 meters. Beating the target's resistance roll by 10+ also reveals Force sensitivity/skill and whether they've met before. |
| **Danger Sense** | Moderate, or the attacker's Control roll | Life Detection | Can be kept up. Gives Graff a round's warning before an attack lands — attackers must declare the action the round before, and Force-sensitive attackers can roll Control to raise the difficulty. |
| **Combat Sense** | Moderate for one opponent, +3 per additional opponent | Danger Sense, Life Detection | No initiative roll needed — Graff acts whenever he chooses. +2D to attack and defense. Lasts 10 rounds; doesn't count as a skill use for die-code penalties. |

*Sources: Concentration, Control Pain, Reduce Injury — [Star Wars: The Roleplaying Game, 2nd Edition Revised & Expanded](../WEG40120.pdf) (WEG40120). Life Detection, Danger Sense, Combat Sense — [Tales of the Jedi Companion](../WEG40082.pdf) (WEG40082).*

*Control Pain and Life Detection were added alongside Reduce Injury and Danger Sense/Combat Sense respectively — both were already implied prerequisites that hadn't been recorded until now.*
